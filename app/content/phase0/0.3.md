## Why this matters

Development boards hide the flashing circuit from you. The ESP32-CAM has none, so you do by hand what the S3 board does automatically: hold a strapping pin, reset, and talk to the ROM bootloader. Once you have done it manually, boot-mode problems on any chip stop being mysterious.

## Prerequisites, hardware, time

- One ESP32-CAM, the FTDI adapter, five jumper wires
- Lesson 0.1 for the toolchain
- 45 minutes

## Build it first

Wire it with everything unplugged from USB. Set the FTDI adapter’s voltage jumper to 3.3 V logic if it has one.

| FTDI adapter | ESP32-CAM | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GND | GND | Always first |
| 5 V (VCC) | 5V | Power the board from 5 V; the 3.3 V pin of most adapters cannot supply enough current |
| TX | U0R (GPIO3) | Crossed: transmit goes to receive |
| RX | U0T (GPIO1) | |
| | IO0 to GND | A jumper wire, only while flashing |

- [ ] Wire as in the table, with IO0 jumpered to GND
- [ ] Plug in USB, then press the board’s reset button once
- [ ] In `firmware/phase0/0.3-blink`, flash. ESP-IDF: `idf.py set-target esp32`, then `idf.py -p PORT flash`. PlatformIO: `pio run -e esp32cam -t upload`
- [ ] Remove the IO0 jumper and press reset
- [ ] Open the serial monitor (`idf.py -p PORT monitor` or `pio device monitor`) and watch the small red LED on the back

```c
/* ESP32-CAM (common AI-Thinker layout): red LED on GPIO33, lit when the pin is LOW. */
#define LED_GPIO   GPIO_NUM_33
#define LED_ON     0

void app_main(void)
{
    gpio_reset_pin(LED_GPIO);
    gpio_set_direction(LED_GPIO, GPIO_MODE_OUTPUT);
    for (int n = 0; ; n++) {
        gpio_set_level(LED_GPIO, (n & 1) ? LED_ON : !LED_ON);
        printf("led %s\n", (n & 1) ? "on" : "off");
        vTaskDelay(pdMS_TO_TICKS(500));
    }
}
```

```expected
led off
led on
led off
```

The same project builds for the S3 (`idf.py set-target esp32s3`), where it drives an external LED on GPIO4.

## How it works

At reset the ROM bootloader samples GPIO0. High, the default thanks to an internal pull-up, means “run the program in flash”. Low means “wait for a new program over the serial port”. The jumper is you holding that pin low.

On your S3 board a USB-serial chip does this for you: the flashing tool toggles two serial control lines (DTR and RTS) that are wired through transistors to the reset and GPIO0 pins. The ESP32-CAM leaves those two transistors out to save space, so you are the transistors.

The LED lights when the pin is **low** because it is wired from 3.3 V through a resistor to the pin: the pin sinks current instead of sourcing it. “Active low” is common and worth a line in every pin table you write.

## Go deeper

- [ ] Leave the IO0 jumper on and press reset. What does the ROM print? This is the message to recognise when a product “will not boot”.
- [ ] Measure the 5 V and 3.3 V pins with a multimeter while the board runs. Record both.
- [ ] Change `LED_GPIO` to `GPIO_NUM_4`, the white flash LED, and its polarity. Do not look into it.

## When to use it, when not to

| Board | Flashing | Choose it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ESP32-S3 dev board | Automatic over USB, with built-in debugging | Learning, and anything needing speed or many pins |
| ESP32-CAM | Manual, through an adapter | A cheap camera node once the code already works |

Develop on the S3. Port to the ESP32-CAM when the feature is done.

## Break it

- [ ] Swap TX and RX and try to flash. Record the exact error.
- [ ] Power the board from the adapter’s 3.3 V pin instead of 5 V and start Wi-Fi later in the course, or simply watch the log now for a brownout message during boot.
- [ ] Flash without the IO0 jumper. Record what the tool reports after its connection attempts.

## Common mistakes and interview angles

- **Brownout resets.** The camera and the radio need more current than a weak USB port or a 3.3 V adapter pin can give. Power from 5 V.
- **Silent monitor.** Some adapters hold the board in reset through DTR or RTS when the monitor opens. `idf.py monitor --help` lists an option to leave those lines alone.
- **Forgetting to remove the jumper.** The board sits in download mode and looks dead.
- **Interview angle.** “A board in the field will not boot. What do you check first?” Supply voltage under load, then the strapping pins’ levels at reset, then the ROM’s first lines.
