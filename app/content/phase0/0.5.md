## Why this matters

“My button triggers by itself” and “the sensor works on my desk but not in the enclosure” are the same fault: a pin that nothing is holding. The other fault this lesson prevents is quieter and permanent: 5 V on a 3.3 V input.

## Prerequisites, hardware, time

A push button, your resistor kit, the HC-SR04, an S3 board. Lesson 0.4. 75 minutes.

## How it works

A digital input does not measure “on” or “off”. It compares a voltage with two thresholds. Above the upper one it reads 1, below the lower one it reads 0, and in between the result is not defined. An unconnected pin has almost infinite resistance, so the tiniest stray current moves its voltage anywhere.

A **pull-up** is a resistor from the pin to the supply. It is weak enough that a button to ground easily overpowers it, and strong enough that stray currents do not. A **pull-down** is the mirror image. The chip has both built in, at tens of kilohms; you enable them in software.

**Logic levels.** Your ESP32 is a 3.3 V device: its pins are not built to tolerate 5 V. The HC-SR04 is powered from 5 V and drives its ECHO pin to 5 V. Two resistors fix that:

::widget calc

With 1 kΩ on top and 2 kΩ on the bottom, 5 V becomes 3.33 V. In the other direction no divider is needed: the 3.3 V the ESP32 sends to TRIG is above the sensor’s threshold for a 1.

This is what that echo pin is telling you, and why its timing is the whole measurement:

::widget echo

**Decoupling and ground.** Every chip draws current in sharp bursts. A 100 nF capacitor next to its supply pin delivers those bursts locally so they do not appear as noise on the rail. And every signal is a voltage measured against ground, so two boards that talk must share a ground wire. Half of all “random” bus errors are a missing ground.

## When to use it, when not to

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Button or switch on a short wire | Internal pull-up, button to ground |
| Long wires, noisy surroundings | External pull-up of 1 kΩ to 10 kΩ, plus a small capacitor |
| I2C lines | External pull-ups always; the internal ones are too weak for the bus speed |
| 5 V output into a 3.3 V input, slow signal | Resistor divider |
| 5 V signals in both directions, or fast | Level-shifter module |

## Common mistakes and interview angles

- **Relying on a pull that is not there.** Some pins have no internal pull resistors; check before relying on one.
- **Forgetting the shared ground.** Two boards, two USB ports, no ground wire between them.
- **Interview angle.** “What is a pull-up for, and how do you choose its value?” Mention defined idle level, the current when driven low, and rise time on a capacitive line.
