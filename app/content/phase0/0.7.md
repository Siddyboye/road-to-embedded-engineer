## Why this matters

Digital signals are analog signals that we agree to read at the right moment. Every “impossible” bug on a wire, the double-counted button or the byte that arrives wrong once a day, lives in the gap between that agreement and the real voltage.

## How it works

**Thresholds.** A wire carries a voltage; the input turns it into 0 or 1 by comparing it with thresholds, as you saw in lesson 0.5. Between them the reading is undefined.

**Edges.** The transition from one level to the other is an edge: rising or falling. Edges are where information and trouble both live. An interrupt “on the falling edge” fires every time the signal crosses downward, however briefly.

**Rise time.** No edge is instant. The wire has capacitance, and whatever drives it has resistance, so the voltage curves toward its new level. A slow edge spends longer in the undefined band. That is why a weak pull-up on a long wire causes errors at speed, and why I2C has a maximum bus capacitance.

**Bounce.** Two pieces of metal closing do not close once. They make and break contact for a few milliseconds, and each one is a perfectly valid edge. The button is not faulty; your assumption was.

**Debouncing.** Accept a new level only after it has held steady for a set time. The cost is latency, as the lab shows. Two other approaches exist and you will meet both: sample the pin on a slow timer instead of using an edge interrupt, or filter in hardware with a resistor and capacitor.

**Glitches.** A glitch is a pulse shorter than anything intended: crosstalk from a neighbouring wire, a spike when a motor switches. You cannot see one by printing. You see it with an instrument that samples much faster than the signal, which is what the logic analyzer is for.

## When to use it, when not to

| Input | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| Human button | Debounce in software, 5 to 20 ms; latency is invisible to a person |
| Rotary encoder, fast | Hardware filter or a peripheral with a glitch filter (lesson 2.8) |
| Signal from another chip | No debounce: it does not bounce. If it glitches, fix the wiring |
| Limit switch on a machine | Debounce, and think about what a missed edge costs |

## Common mistakes and interview angles

- **Debouncing with a delay inside the interrupt handler.** It blocks everything else. Record a timestamp and decide later.
- **Assuming all switches bounce alike.** They range from under a millisecond to tens.
- **Interview angle.** “Debounce a button” is a standard whiteboard task. Say what you are trading (latency for certainty), then give a timer-based version.
