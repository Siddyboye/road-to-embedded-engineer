## Why this matters

The datasheet is the contract and the schematic is the map. Tutorials are someone’s memory of both. The engineers who are trusted with hardware are the ones who go to the source, and it is a learnable skill: you are looking for about six tables.

## Prerequisites, hardware, time

Your S3 board and a notebook or spreadsheet. The ESP32-S3 Series Datasheet, the ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 user guide, and the MPU6050 datasheet, all free from the manufacturers. 90 minutes.

## Build it first

You are building the one document you will open in every later lesson: your own pin table. Your board is probably a clone, so the silkscreen on it outranks any diagram online.

- [ ] List every pin label printed on your board, in order, both sides
- [ ] Mark the pins the module uses for flash and PSRAM. On N16R8 that includes GPIO35, 36 and 37
- [ ] Mark the two USB pins, GPIO19 and GPIO20
- [ ] Mark the four strapping pins. Find them in the datasheet section named “Strapping Pins”
- [ ] Mark the UART0 pins used by the USB-serial bridge
- [ ] Find which pin drives the on-board RGB LED. It is printed on some boards; otherwise it is GPIO48 or GPIO38
- [ ] Choose eight “safe first choice” pins and highlight them

Compare your table with the course’s [pin warnings cheatsheet](#sheets-s3-pins). Where they differ, your board wins; note the difference.

## How it works

**What a datasheet contains, in the order you will use it.**

| Section | Answers | You used or will use it in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Features, block diagram | What is this part, in one page | 0.1 |
| Pin description | What each pin can do; which have special roles at reset | This lesson |
| Absolute maximum ratings | What destroys it | 0.5 |
| Recommended operating conditions | What it is guaranteed to work at | Every power decision |
| DC characteristics | Thresholds, drive current, leakage | 0.4, 0.5 |
| Timing diagrams and tables | How fast, and in what order | All of Phase 3 |
| Register map | Every bit you can read or write | 0.13, and every driver you write |

Absolute maximum is not a target. A part run at its absolute maximum is being damaged slowly. Design to the recommended conditions.

**What a schematic contains.** Symbols for parts, lines for wires, and names on wires: two wires with the same net name are connected even when no line joins them. Power symbols (3V3, GND) are net names too. Read a schematic by following one signal from the connector to the chip and naming every part it touches.

**The chip has more than one document.** The datasheet covers electrical facts and pins. The Technical Reference Manual covers how each peripheral works and its registers. The module datasheet covers the metal can on your board: which flash, which PSRAM, which pins those consume. The errata lists the bugs in the silicon. Knowing which document answers which question is half the skill.

## Go deeper

Answer each from the source and write down the page number. The page number is the point: it is how you will cite a fact in a design review.

- [ ] MPU6050: what is its I2C address, and which pin changes it?
- [ ] MPU6050: what supply voltage range does it accept? Is it safe on 3.3 V?
- [ ] MPU6050: which register do you read to confirm you are talking to the right chip, and what value should it return?
- [ ] ESP32-S3: what is the absolute maximum voltage on an input pin?
- [ ] DevKitC-1 schematic: follow the BOOT button. Which pin does it pull, and to what?

## When to use it, when not to

| You want | Open |
| --- | --- |
| A pin’s electrical limits | Chip datasheet |
| How to configure a timer or what a register bit does | Technical Reference Manual |
| Which pins the memory uses on your module | Module datasheet |
| Why a documented feature misbehaves | Errata |
| A quick wiring picture | A tutorial, then verify it against the above |

## Break it

- [ ] Find a pinout diagram for “ESP32-S3 N16R8” from an image search and compare it with your table. List every disagreement. There is usually at least one.

## Check yourself

1. Two wires on a schematic do not touch but share a name. Are they connected?
2. What is the difference between “absolute maximum” and “recommended operating” voltage?
3. Which document tells you what bit 3 of a control register does?

## Common mistakes and interview angles

- **Trusting a diagram for a different board revision.** The LED pin moved between DevKitC-1 revisions; clones vary more.
- **Reading “typical” as “guaranteed”.** Design to the minimum and maximum columns.
- **Interview angle.** You may be handed a datasheet and asked for one number. Being quick and saying where you found it is the whole test.
